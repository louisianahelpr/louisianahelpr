# Lease comparison: the owner's three Louisiana residential leases

> **Not legal advice; based on the primary sources cited; review before use.**

Researched 2026-10-14. Every law marked **VERIFIED** below was read in full on the official source (legis.la.gov, eCFR, or govinfo.gov U.S. Code) on that date. Anything not read is marked **NOT VERIFIED**. No personal details from the leases appear here. The leases are called:

- **Lease A**: the McClellan file (9 pages, plus a flood/environmental addendum and a payment schedule)
- **Lease B**: the Maude file (7 pages, plus a lead-paint page)
- **Lease C**: the Railroad file (7 pages, plus a lead-paint page)

All three were e-signed and have a signing-service audit trail on the last page.

## 1. Are they the same template?

**B and C are the same form, almost word for word.** They have the same 43 numbered sections in the same order with the same wording, and the same incomplete lead-paint page. Only the filled-in blanks differ (parties, property, rent, deposit, occupants), and B has a "due at signing" box that C does not. This is a common generic online lease form. It is not written for Louisiana: for example, it refers to "county" ordinances, and its governing-law clause says "the State where the Premises is located."

**A was clearly built from that same form, then rewritten for Louisiana.** About two-thirds of A's clauses match a B/C clause in subject and order (property, term, rent, deposit, utilities, occupants, use, furnishings, appliances, late fee, move-in inspection, sale of property, smoking, pets, maintenance, hazardous materials, entry, subletting, default, waiver, indemnity, severability, entire agreement). A rewrites them in plainer words with Louisiana citations, and adds about a dozen protections B/C lack.

## 2. Which is best, and why

**Lease A is clearly the best base.** It is the same skeleton as B/C, rewritten for Louisiana:

- **It already has the legally required foreclosure disclosure** (R.S. 9:3260.1). B and C do not.
- It gets the deposit rules right: one month, itemized deductions, forwarding address, and only "to the extent permitted" on forfeiture.
- It cites the correct Civil Code repair articles. It keeps the "as-is" appliance exception subject to habitability.
- It preserves the tenant's legal rights to leave early (military and domestic abuse) instead of overriding them with a fee.
- It treats assistance animals correctly.
- It has a lawful waiver of the 5-day notice to vacate. That makes evictions faster, and Louisiana allows it.
- It has a real storm and casualty clause, freeze rules, a duty to report leaks and mold, and a flood addendum.
- Its indemnity clause carves out the landlord's negligence, so it is much closer to Civil Code art. 2004 than B/C's.

B/C add a few small things worth keeping: the keys/access clause, the landlord's failure-to-deliver clause (the tenant may cancel and get everything back), and the specific house rules.

### 3b. Clauses in the current leases that look unenforceable or illegal in Louisiana

Ranked by how serious they are. "Likely" means my reading of the statute; I found no court case applying it to these exact words.

1. **B, C: no foreclosure disclosure.** This is a required disclosure that is missing, not a bad clause. R.S. 9:3260.1(A) requires it "during, and prior to entering into" every residential lease, with a $200 penalty (9:3260.1(D)). A has it.
2. **A, B, C: tenant replaces smoke-detector batteries.** R.S. 40:1581 requires a **ten-year sealed lithium-battery** smoke detector and a **sealed-battery** CO detector in every one- or two-family home at the time of lease. Sealed units have no battery to replace. A battery-swap clause signals the house may have the wrong detectors. B and C don't mention CO detectors at all.
3. **B, C §22: "after 7 days the Landlord may … remove all belongings."** CCP 4731(B) lets a landlord retake possession without court only *after the required notice* and on a reasonable belief of abandonment. In a parish under a federal disaster declaration, an absence in the first 30 days cannot count as abandonment (4731(C)(1)). A violation costs $500 or 2× monthly rent (4731(C)(2)). As written, this clause invites an illegal lockout.
4. **A §22, B, C §29: evict AND demand all remaining rent.** Louisiana treats these as mutually exclusive (Lobell, 4th Cir. 2018, quoting Richard v. Broussard, La. 1986). Once the landlord cancels and evicts, future rent is forfeited. The clause won't be enforced as written.
5. **B, C §38: landlord not liable "unless caused *solely* by the Landlord's negligence."** This is **null** to the extent it limits liability for physical injury or gross fault (Civ. Code art. 2004). A's version, which carves out negligence and adds "to the extent permitted by Louisiana law," is close. It still would not shield a non-negligent injury claim under the warranty (arts. 2696-2697), unless the lease uses the R.S. 9:3221 tenant-responsibility clause.
6. **B, C §4: "to receive a security deposit back, the tenant must give 30 days notice before vacating."** At the natural end of a fixed term the lease ends "without need of notice" (art. 2720). Forfeiting the deposit for no notice then would be a waiver of the R.S. 9:3251 right, which 9:3254 makes void. It only works for true abandonment before the lease ends (9:3251(C)). **Likely unenforceable** except in that case.
7. **B, C §15: early-termination fee with no exception for military or sexual-assault terminations.** Charging it in those cases violates 50 U.S.C. 3955(e) ("may not impose an early termination charge") and R.S. 9:3261(D)-(E) (at most 1 month's rent, deposit back, cannot be waived). R.S. 9:3261.2(E) also cannot be waived. The clause needs a carve-out.
8. **B, C §36 "Equal Housing."** It says the landlord provides modifications "unless … too difficult or expensive." Federal law says modifications are **at the tenant's expense** and the landlord must permit reasonable ones (42 U.S.C. 3604(f)(3)(A)). The "too difficult" idea belongs to rule *accommodations* (3604(f)(3)(B)). The clause also "encourages" tenants to disclose impairments; asking applicants about disability is barred (24 CFR 100.202(c)). Rewrite it.
9. **B, C §25 and A §9: "no warranty" on the AC and other appliances.** A waiver works only in "clear and unambiguous language that is brought to the attention of the lessee," and never for defects that seriously affect health or safety in a residential lease (art. 2699). B/C's is buried mid-paragraph in a list of appliances "if the Premises includes" them, so **likely ineffective.** A's is better (it carves out habitability) but is still not separately signed or initialed.
10. **B, C §19: "by taking possession [Tenant] … accepted the Premises in good order."** This cannot waive defects the landlord knew or should have known about, or health and safety defects (art. 2699(1), (3)). It is ineffective to that extent. Use a signed move-in checklist instead.
11. **A, B, C: uncapped daily late fees.** The fee is legal in principle (stipulated damages, arts. 2005-2012), but a per-day fee with no ceiling can reach a large share of a month's rent. A court may cut it if it is "so manifestly unreasonable as to be contrary to public policy" (art. 2012). B/C start it the day after the due date with no grace period. **Risky, not illegal per se.**
12. **B, C §29: default for breaking "rules … that may be hereafter prescribed by the Landlord."** A lease is law between the parties and changes only by consent (art. 1983, my reading). New rules the tenant never signed are not part of the lease. Also, "arrested" alone (not convicted) as a default trigger is a fair-housing risk. NOT VERIFIED (the current federal guidance was not checked), so the template uses "criminal activity on the premises" instead.
13. **A §27, B, C §18: notices by phone or text.** These are fine for repair requests. But default and eviction notices for a primary residence are outside Louisiana's e-signature law (R.S. 9:2603(B)(4)(b)(ii)), and termination notices must be in writing (art. 2729). Keep a paper notice address.
14. **A §29, B, C lead-paint pages** (only if the home was built before 1978, which I can't tell from the leases):
    - **A** paraphrases instead of using the **required Lead Warning Statement text** (40 CFR 745.113(b)(1)). It gives a web link instead of the pamphlet (745.107(a)(1) says "provide"). It has no separate tenant acknowledgment line.
    - **B/C** use near-exact wording (with "landlords"/"Tenants" swapped in for "lessors"/"Lessees"), but **skip the tenant's acknowledgment of receiving the information and pamphlet** (745.113(b)(4)). The page jumps from item 2 to item 5.

**Lawful and worth keeping:** A's **waiver of the 5-day notice to vacate** (CCP 4701 ¶3, 4731(A)). One federal caveat: if the property has a federally backed mortgage, the CARES Act 30-day notice (15 U.S.C. 9058(c)) may still apply, so check the mortgage before relying on the waiver for a nonpayment eviction.

## 4. Changes from your lease (plain words)

These are the changes made in the new blank template (`lease-template-draft.md`), compared with Lease A, the one it is built from. Items marked (B/C) came from, or fix, Leases B and C.

### Removed

1. **"Landlord can evict you AND make you pay the rest of the lease."** Louisiana courts make the landlord pick one. The template says the landlord may (a) end the lease and evict, keeping the right to past-due rent and damage costs, or (b) keep the lease going and collect the rent owed.
2. **Tenant changes smoke-detector batteries.** Louisiana requires 10-year sealed detectors (smoke) and a sealed CO detector. The landlord now installs those. The tenant tests them monthly, doesn't remove them, and reports problems.
3. **Uncapped "$X every day" late fee.** It is replaced by one late fee after a grace period, with an optional small daily fee and a monthly maximum you choose. That makes it much harder for a judge to throw out.
4. (B/C) **"Give 30 days' notice or lose your deposit."** It doesn't hold up at the normal end of a lease. Only abandonment before the lease ends affects the deposit.
5. (B/C) **"After 7 days we can clear out your stuff."** It is replaced by Louisiana's real rule: post a notice first, have clear signs of abandonment, and wait 30 days after a federal disaster declaration.
6. (B/C) **"Not liable unless *solely* our fault."** Louisiana voids clauses that dodge liability for injuries or gross fault. The new clause is limited to what the law allows.
7. (B/C) **"You accepted the house in good order."** It is replaced by the signed move-in checklist.
8. (B/C) **"Rules the Landlord makes later."** Only rules in the lease, or that both sides sign later, count.

### Added

9. **Correct lead-paint addendum** (pre-1978 homes): the required federal warning in its exact words, the landlord's disclosure, the records list, the tenant's signed receipt of the pamphlet, and signatures and dates.
10. **Sexual-assault early-termination right** (R.S. 9:3261.2), next to the military and domestic-abuse rights. None of these comes with a fee.
11. **Paper notices for defaults and evictions.** Texts and email are still fine for everyday matters and repairs.
12. **Deposit rules spelled out:** one month to return it, an itemized list of anything kept (by the new 2026 deadline at the latest), what happens if the house is sold (the deposit goes to the new owner), and the tenant's forwarding address.
13. **Foreclosure follow-up:** if a foreclosure seizure is ever served, the tenants get the required written notice within 7 days, using the statute's wording.
14. **Pet and assistance-animal addendum:** pet fees and limits apply to pets only. Assistance animals have no pet fee, deposit or weight limit, but the tenant still pays for real damage.
15. **Move-in/move-out checklist form**, room by room, signed by both sides. All three old leases mentioned a checklist but none included one.
16. **Optional "tenant takes responsibility for the condition" clause** (R.S. 9:3221). It limits the landlord's injury liability to defects the landlord knew or should have known about. It is optional and must be initialed.
17. **"As-is" appliances as a separate initialed box.** This is the only way such a waiver can work, and it never covers health or safety problems.
18. **Month-to-month and reconduction explained:** a fixed lease ends on its own date. If the tenant stays a week past it without either side objecting in writing, the lease rolls into month-to-month. Either side then ends it with 30 days' written notice; the law's minimum is 10 days.
19. (B/C) **Kept from B/C:** if the landlord can't hand over the house on time, the tenant may cancel and get everything back; the keys clause; and the "call the landlord before repairing" rule (adjusted so it doesn't take away the tenant's legal repair right).
20. **CARES Act check box:** if the property has a federally backed mortgage, give a 30-day notice to vacate.
21. **Application-fee notice** (for the app): if a fee is charged, show the required notice first (R.S. 9:3258.1).

### Changed

22. **Equal housing clause** rewritten to match federal law: tenants may ask for an accommodation or modification at any time, and modifications are at the tenant's cost with restoration.
23. **Early termination by the tenant** is now an option you turn on or off, and it never applies to military, abuse or assault terminations.
24. **Sale of the house** clause kept, with a note that Louisiana's 2026 law change says a sale doesn't end the lease and the tenant may claim losses from the landlord.
25. **Notice-to-vacate waiver** kept (it is legal), with its own initial line so no one can say it was hidden.

### Top 10 changes, if you only read ten

1. Fix the smoke and CO detectors: sealed 10-year units, installed by the landlord (R.S. 40:1581).
2. Remove "evict AND collect all future rent." Pick one.
3. Cap the late fee.
4. Fix the lead-paint page: exact federal wording, a tenant receipt line, and a copy of the pamphlet.
5. Add the sexual-assault early-termination right (R.S. 9:3261.2). No fee for military, abuse or assault terminations.
6. Send default and eviction notices on paper, not by text.
7. Follow Louisiana's abandonment rule before touching a tenant's belongings (CCP 4731).
8. Make the "as-is appliance" waiver a separately initialed box that excludes health and safety.
9. Deposit: one month to return it, an itemized list (deadline extended by 15 days in 2026), the transfer-on-sale rule, and no forfeiture at a normal move-out.
10. Add the pet/assistance-animal addendum and a real move-in/move-out checklist.
