## Legal findings (2026-10-14) — lawyer needed before build
- VERIFIED by reading the statute (legis.la.gov): La. R.S. 37:3103 defines "auction" to include sales by competitive bid over the internet, incl. "Online auctions" and "Extended auctions"; an "auction business" runs 2+ auctions in 12 months, explicitly incl. an "internet-based platform" (a company giving people an online forum to sell their own property "via the submission of silent bids"). R.S. 37:3105(A): public auctions must be conducted by a licensed auctioneer / licensed auction house; (C) online-only auction houses need not employ an auctioneer to CALL the auction; (D) the platform is "responsible for the actions of that platform". R.S. 37:3104 exempts an individual selling their own property (not on a regular basis) and "a sale conducted by sealed bid".
- VERIFIED 2026-10-14 (lalb.org homepage): "Effective January 1, 2021, Act 327 ... will require ALL online auctions to be properly licensed by this Board if the auction or item being sold at auction is located in Louisiana." Current R.S. 37:3103(4) has NO exclusion for internet trading platforms (only livestock).
- VERIFIED 2026-10-14 (Cornell copy of LAC 46:III.1101 and 1120): selling your own property at auction more than once in a rolling 12 months = in the business, license required (1101.D(4)(a)); sealed-bid exclusion covers silent auctions "unless there is an active solicitation of individual bids" (1101.D(7)); auction business license $300/yr, renew by Feb 1, bond (Cornell copy: $10,000), two licensed-auctioneer references, "LA" license number on the website and ads; internet-only houses need no auctioneer to call the auction.
- Comparisons: Nellis operates in Nevada and Arizona (third-party guides say neither state licenses auctioneers statewide; not verified), so Nellis tells us nothing about Louisiana. Vinted is fixed price + offers, not an auction, so auction law does not reach it.
- VERIFIED 2026-10-14 (legis.la.gov R.S. 37:3113(B), 37:3118(A)(1)): an auction business must employ a licensed auctioneer and name a licensed Louisiana auctioneer as its "qualifying party" (sole proprietor or spouse, 120-day full-time employee, officer/stockholder/director, partner, or LLC member/manager); auction business bond $25,000 cash or surety (auctioneer $10,000). The Cornell copy of rule 1120 still says $10,000: the statute's $25,000 governs.
- Agent reading, superseded by the verified lines above of the Jan-2026 rule book PDF, which I could not decode) of the board's Jan-2026 rule book PDF, which I could not decode): rule LAC 46:III.1101.D treats selling your own property at auction more than once in a rolling 12 months as being in the business; sealed-bid exemption lost with "active solicitation of individual bids"; auction business license = $300/yr, $25,000 bond, a licensed Louisiana auctioneer as "qualifying party".
- Other checks (agent research, primary sources cited, not re-read by me): sales tax (marketplace facilitator over $100k/yr; occasional-sale exemption unclear when a marketplace collects; premium probably taxable; separately stated optional delivery likely not taxable); Apple 3.1.3(e) requires non-IAP payment for physical goods, 2.3.1(a) forbids hidden features in a review build unless described in Review Notes; Stripe: ordinary auctions allowed, bidding-fee/penny auctions prohibited, don't call it "escrow"; CPSC: reselling recalled products is illegal, pre-2011 cribs illegal to resell; INFORM Act only counts new/unused items; Louisiana secondhand-dealer law (R.S. 37:1861) may reach very frequent sellers.
- VERIFIED 2026-10-14: Louisiana penalties R.S. 37:3123(B): up to $500 fine and/or 6 months' prison "for each such violation", each a separate offense; R.S. 37:3122: board cease-and-desist, then court TRO/injunction after 10 days; the board must investigate any written complaint. No published enforcement case against an online platform found (not proof there are none).
- VERIFIED 2026-10-14 (texas.public.law, Tex. Occ. Code §1802.002(a)(14)): Texas exempts "an auction of property through the Internet" — that's why online auctions run freely in Texas. Louisiana did the opposite with Act 327 (2020).
- Owner asked (2026-10-14) whether calling bids "offers" avoids the law: no — R.S. 37:3103's definition of auction is itself "a series of invitations for offers"; the law follows how the sale works, not its labels.
- Design options outside R.S. 37:3103 (all three elements needed: competitive bid + series of invitations for offers + acceptance of the highest OR MOST FAVORABLE bid), safest first: (1) seller-accepts offers, first accepted + paid wins (Mercari/OfferUp model); (2) slow automatic daily price drops to a seller floor, first to Buy wins (Poshmark/Mercari) — avoid a fast countdown, which is a "Dutch auction"; (3) true sealed bid, one unchangeable hidden offer, no alerts (R.S. 37:3104 exemption; board rule 1101.D(7) "active solicitation" caveat) — lawyer's written OK only; (4) partner with a licensed Louisiana auction company that conducts the auctions in our app (R.S. 37:3105(B)) — lawyer-written contract. NOT a way out: renaming bids "offers"; seller choosing the best of a 24 h offer window ("most favorable" still matches).
- CORRECTION 2026-10-14: the auction-business bond is $10,000 or $25,000 — a board source says $25,000 is for auction businesses selling REAL ESTATE (effective 2025); the statute text read earlier didn't show that limit. Ask the board.
- Finding an auctioneer (2026-10-14): board contact (public) Sandy Edmonds, exec. director, (225) 295-8420, admin@lalb.org; state license lookup via la.gov online services; licensed LA auction companies listed on AuctionZip (auctionzip.com/la.html). - B18. Draft edit: `declutter_update_draft` takes every field create takes (seller's own draft only). Changing the

- B19. AI (row 32/34): edge function `declutter-ai-draft` (behind requireFeature + confirmed email + per-IP limit),
