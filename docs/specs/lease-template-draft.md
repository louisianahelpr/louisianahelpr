# Louisiana Residential Lease: improved blank template (DRAFT)

> **Not legal advice; based on the primary sources cited; review before use.**

Draft of 2026-10-14 for a future in-app Rentals feature. It is built from the owner's best lease ("Lease A") plus the protections the other two leases and the law call for. Every blank is in **[BRACKETS]**. "Choose one" options are marked ☐. Each section ends with a short *Legal basis* note citing a law read on its official source (see `lease-comparison.md` for links and what each law says). Where something was not verified it says so.

**How to use it:** fill every bracket. Delete any option you do not choose. Have every party initial the boxes marked **INITIAL**. Sign. Give the tenant a full copy, including every addendum.

## RESIDENTIAL LEASE AGREEMENT (State of Louisiana)

This lease is made on **[DATE]** between **[LANDLORD FULL LEGAL NAME / BUSINESS NAME]** ("Landlord") and **[TENANT FULL NAME(S)]** ("Tenant"; if more than one, each of them). Landlord and Tenant agree as follows. This lease is governed by Louisiana law, including the Louisiana Civil Code articles on lease (arts. 2668-2729) and La. R.S. 9:3251-3261.2.

*Legal basis: Civ. Code art. 2668 defines a lease. Art. 2669 sends anything the lease title doesn't cover to the general contract rules.*

### 1. The home

Landlord leases to Tenant the home at **[STREET ADDRESS, CITY, LA ZIP]** (the "Home"): a **[house / duplex unit / apartment]** with **[#]** bedrooms and **[#]** bathrooms, including **[yard / driveway / storage / other]**. The Home is for use as a private residence only.

*Legal basis: art. 2683(2) says the tenant uses the home as a prudent administrator and for its agreed purpose. Art. 2686 covers misuse.*

### 2. Term

☐ **Fixed term:** begins **[START DATE]** and ends **[END DATE]**. The lease ends on the end date automatically; no notice is needed.
☐ **Month-to-month:** begins **[START DATE]** and continues month to month.

**Early move-in (optional):** Tenant may move in on **[DATE]**. Rent for those days is **[$AMOUNT / none]**.

**After a fixed term ends:** if Tenant stays more than one week after the end date and neither party has objected in writing, the lease continues month to month on these same terms. Either party may end a month-to-month lease with written notice at least **[30]** days before the end of a monthly period. To prevent the automatic month-to-month, either party may give written notice before the end date that the lease will not continue.

*Legal basis: art. 2720 (a fixed term ends without notice). Arts. 2721 and 2723-2724 (staying one week past the term without opposition reconducts the lease month to month). Arts. 2727-2729 (month-to-month termination needs written notice at least 10 days before the end of the month; a longer agreed period is fine).*

### 3. Rent

Rent is **[$AMOUNT]** per month, due in advance on the **[1st]** day of each month. Pay by **[METHOD(S)]** to **[PAYEE / ACCOUNT LABEL; never put bank numbers in the lease]**. The first month's rent of **[$AMOUNT]** is due **[at signing / on DATE]**. Rent for a partial first month is **[prorated daily / $AMOUNT]**. See the Payment Schedule at the end.

*Legal basis: arts. 2683(1) and 2703 (rent is due at the start of each period unless agreed otherwise; paid at the address the landlord gives). Art. 2676 (rent must be a sum certain or determinable).*

### 4. Late fee and returned payments

Rent is late if not received in full by the end of the **[5th]** day of the month. A late fee of **[$AMOUNT]** is then due. ☐ Starting the **[6th]** day, an additional **[$AMOUNT]** per day is due until rent is paid. **The total late fees for any one month will never exceed [$AMOUNT or __% of one month's rent].**

A payment returned unpaid (for example, a bounced check or reversed transfer) incurs a fee of **[$25 or 5% of the payment, whichever is greater; for a check this is the legal maximum]**, plus any late fee.

*Legal basis: late fees are "stipulated damages" (arts. 2005-2012). A fixed due date puts the tenant in default without extra notice (art. 1990). A court may reduce a fee only if it is "so manifestly unreasonable as to be contrary to public policy" (art. 2012), so a cap is strongly recommended. No Louisiana statute capping residential late fees was found (not verified as a negative). Bounced-check fee: R.S. 9:2782(B).*

### 5. Security deposit

Tenant pays a refundable security deposit of **[$AMOUNT]**, due **[at signing / in installments per the Payment Schedule]**.

- **Return:** Landlord returns the deposit within **one month** after the lease ends and Tenant gives back possession and keys.
- **Deductions:** Landlord may keep only what is reasonably needed to cover unpaid rent or other amounts Tenant owes under this lease, or damage beyond normal wear and tear. If Landlord keeps any part, Landlord sends an **itemized written list** of what was kept and why, within one month after the lease ends (Louisiana law allows up to 15 more days after that month).
- **Forwarding address:** Tenant gives Landlord a forwarding address in writing at move-out.
- **Not last month's rent:** Tenant may not use the deposit as rent unless Landlord agrees in writing.
- **Abandonment:** if Tenant abandons the Home before the lease ends, or without giving the notice this lease requires, the one-month return rule does not apply. Landlord will still account for the deposit.
- **Sale of the Home:** if the Home is sold, Landlord transfers the deposit to the new owner, who becomes responsible for returning it.

*Legal basis: R.S. 9:3251(A)-(C) (one month; itemized statement within one month or 15 days after that, per Acts 2026, No. 63; forwarding address; transfer on sale; abandonment exception). R.S. 9:3252 (willful failure: the deposit plus $300 or twice the amount withheld, whichever is greater; 30 days after written demand counts as willful). R.S. 9:3253 (attorney fees). R.S. 9:3254 (tenant's deposit rights cannot be waived).*

### 6. Utilities and services

Tenant pays for and puts in Tenant's name, effective on move-in: **[electric / gas / water / sewer / trash / internet / other]**. Landlord pays for: **[none / list]**. Tenant keeps utilities on through move-out, including heat in freezing weather.

*Legal basis: a contract term (art. 1983). Art. 2689 (the landlord pays property taxes and charges on the thing, except those arising from the tenant's use).*

### 7. Who lives here; guests

Only Tenant and these occupants may live in the Home: **[OCCUPANT NAMES]**. A guest may stay no more than **[14]** days in a row or **[__]** days in any 6 months without Landlord's written OK, which Landlord will not unreasonably refuse.

Emergency contact (not living in the Home): **[NAME, PHONE]**.

*Legal basis: a contract term. Fair Housing Act, 42 U.S.C. 3604(c): no term may show a preference based on familial status or another protected class. This draft asks for names only, not ages.*

### 8. Furnishings and appliances

The Home is **[unfurnished / furnished: list]**. Landlord provides these appliances, which stay Landlord's property: **[LIST]**. Tenant supplies: **[LIST]**.

**Optional "as-is" appliances (INITIAL only if used): ______ / ______**
Landlord does not promise to repair or replace these specific items if they fail: **[LIST, e.g., washer, dryer, window AC unit]**. **This does NOT apply** to any problem that seriously affects health or safety, or to any defect Landlord knew or should have known about. Landlord remains responsible for everything else in Section 12.

*Legal basis: art. 2699. A waiver of the warranty against defects works only if it is "clear and unambiguous" and "brought to the attention of the lessee," which is why it is separately initialed. It is never effective for defects the landlord knew or should have known about, for anything art. 2004 forbids, or, in a residential lease, for defects that seriously affect health or safety.*

### 9. Move-in condition and possession

Within **[5]** days after move-in, Landlord and Tenant together complete and sign the **Move-In/Move-Out Condition Checklist (Addendum 3)**, and each keeps a copy. It is used at move-out to decide deposit deductions.

If Landlord cannot deliver the Home on the start date, Tenant may cancel this lease in writing and get back all money paid, including any deposit, prepaid rent and application fee.

*Legal basis: arts. 2682(1) and 2684 (the landlord delivers on time and in good condition). Art. 2683(3) (the tenant returns the home in the same condition, except normal wear). Kept from Leases B/C §19. The "tenant accepted the premises in good order" sentence was removed: under art. 2699 it cannot waive known defects or health and safety defects.*

### 10. Pets and assistance animals

☐ **No pets.** ☐ **Pets allowed per the Pet & Assistance-Animal Addendum (Addendum 2).**
An assistance animal (a service animal or emotional-support animal needed because of a disability) **is not a pet.** Pet rules, pet fees and pet limits do not apply to it. See Addendum 2.

*Legal basis: 42 U.S.C. 3604(f)(3)(B) and 24 CFR 100.204 (refusing a reasonable accommodation, such as waiving a no-pet rule for a needed animal, is discrimination; see the regulation's guide-dog example).*

### 11. House rules

No smoking or vaping inside the Home **[or anywhere on the property]**. No waterbeds. No swimming pools or trampolines. No dangerous amounts of gasoline, propane, kerosene, fireworks or similar (normal household and grill amounts are fine). No satellite dish mounted on the house (ground mount only). No stickers, nails or screws in walls without written OK (**[small picture hooks are fine]**). Do not flush wipes or pour grease down drains. No inoperable or unregistered vehicles or junk piles in the yard. Tenant follows noise and other local laws and creates no nuisance. Additional rules: **[LIST, or "None"]**. New rules apply only if both parties sign them.

*Legal basis: arts. 2683(2) and 2686. Art. 1983 (a contract binds as written and changes by consent; my reading is that rules added later without the tenant's agreement are not part of the lease).*

### 12. Repairs and upkeep

**Landlord** keeps the Home in a condition fit for living in and makes all repairs needed for that, except those Tenant causes. This includes the roof, structure, plumbing, electrical, heating, water heater, **[air conditioning]**, and the appliances Landlord provides (subject to any initialed as-is list in Section 8).

**Tenant**:
(a) keeps the Home clean and sanitary;
(b) pays to repair damage caused by Tenant, occupants, guests or pets, beyond normal wear and tear;
(c) clears drain or toilet clogs Tenant causes;
(d) **[mows the lawn at least every __ days / Landlord provides lawn care]**;
(e) replaces HVAC filters every **[30]** days (Landlord supplies them: ☐ yes ☐ no);
(f) replaces light bulbs;
(g) does routine pest control: ☐ Tenant ☐ Landlord;
(h) takes trash out on collection days;
(i) keeps the Home reasonably ventilated, and in freezing weather drips faucets and covers outdoor spigots;
(j) **reports in writing, without delay,** any leak, water intrusion, mold, damage or needed repair, and is responsible for extra damage caused by not reporting.

**How repairs happen:** Tenant asks Landlord first (Section 21) and does not hire someone without asking, except in an emergency that threatens health, safety or serious property damage when Landlord cannot be reached. Landlord will repair within a reasonable time after Tenant's request. This lease does not take away any repair right Louisiana law gives Tenant if Landlord fails to repair within a reasonable time after demand.

**Alterations:** none without Landlord's written OK.

*Legal basis: arts. 2682(2) and 2691 (the landlord's repair duty). Arts. 2687 and 2692 (the tenant pays for damage they or their guests cause). Art. 2688 (the tenant must report without delay; the landlord may recover damages caused by a failure to report). Art. 2693 (urgent repairs; rent abatement). Art. 2694 (the tenant's right to repair and deduct after demand; whether a lease can waive it was not verified, so this draft does not try). Art. 2695 (improvements).*

### 13. Smoke and carbon monoxide detectors

Before move-in, Landlord installs at least one working **ten-year sealed lithium-battery smoke detector** and at least one working **sealed long-life-battery carbon monoxide detector** (a combination unit is fine). Locations: **[LIST]**. Tenant tests them monthly, never removes or disables them, and reports any failure to Landlord in writing right away. Landlord replaces any unit that fails.

*Legal basis: R.S. 40:1581(A)-(B). Every existing one- or two-family dwelling, at the time of lease, must contain those detectors. For buildings of three or more units, check fire-code rules; that was not verified here. The old clause making the tenant change batteries was removed because sealed units have no replaceable battery.*

### 14. Landlord entry; locks

Landlord may enter with at least **[24]** hours' notice, at reasonable times, to inspect, repair, or show the Home to buyers, lenders or new tenants. Landlord may enter without notice in an emergency. Tenant does not add or change locks without Landlord's written OK, and gives Landlord a key or code to any approved new lock. Landlord gives Tenant **[#]** keys, fobs or codes. Replacements cost **[$AMOUNT]** each. All keys are returned at move-out, or the replacement cost is deducted.

*Legal basis: no Louisiana statute on landlord entry was found (not verified as a negative), so this is a contract term. Art. 2682(3) (peaceful possession) and art. 2690 (no alterations by the landlord during the lease) limit entry to legitimate purposes. Keys clause kept from Leases B/C §20.*

### 15. Subletting and assignment

Tenant may not sublet, assign, or rent out any part of the Home (including short-term rentals) without Landlord's prior written consent. Consent once is not consent again.

*Legal basis: art. 2713 (a lease may prohibit these; prohibiting one prohibits the others unless stated otherwise; the prohibition is read strictly against the landlord). Art. 2713.1 (a sublessee gets no greater rights; Acts 2026, No. 240).*

### 16. Sale of the Home

If Landlord sells the Home, Landlord tells Tenant the new owner's or manager's contact information. ☐ (Optional) The new owner may end this lease by written notice to Tenant at least **[30]** days in advance, and Tenant's deposit and any prepaid rent are then handled under Section 5.

*Legal basis: art. 2711 (a sale does not end the lease, and the tenant has an action against the landlord for loss caused by the transfer). Art. 2712 (a buyer is not bound by the lease unless the buyer assumes it). Art. 2681.1 (a lease of an immovable binds third persons from the time it is filed for registry). Act 240 of 2026 rewrote these articles; its official comment says it "does not change the law." R.S. 9:3251(B) (the deposit moves to the buyer). Whether the optional clause limits the tenant's art. 2711 claim against the landlord was not verified.*

### 17. Ending the lease early

**Tenant's legal rights (always available, never with a fee):**
- **Military:** an active or reserve servicemember (or their spouse) may end the lease under the federal Servicemembers Civil Relief Act (50 U.S.C. 3955) or La. R.S. 9:3261 (for example, change-of-station or deployment orders, discharge, or government quarters). They give written notice with a copy of the orders. No early termination charge applies.
- **Sexual assault:** a tenant who is a victim may end the lease under La. R.S. 9:3261.2 with the documentation that law requires.
- **Domestic abuse:** where La. R.S. 9:3261.1 applies (buildings of 6 or more units, with exceptions), a victim may end the lease under that law. This lease never limits or penalizes anyone for calling police or emergency help.

**Optional paid early exit (☐ use / ☐ don't use):** Tenant may end a fixed-term lease early by giving at least **[30]** days' written notice and paying an early-termination fee of **[$AMOUNT]**, plus rent through the move-out date. This option never applies to the legal rights listed above.

*Legal basis: 50 U.S.C. 3955(a), (c), (d) and (e) (no early termination charge; rent prorated). R.S. 9:3261(D)-(E) (at most 1 month's rent, or ½ month after 6 months; full deposit back; cannot be waived). R.S. 9:3261.1(C) (no lease may limit or penalize summoning police). R.S. 9:3261.2(E) (cannot be waived). Art. 2718 (a notice period reserved in a lease cannot be renounced in advance).*

### 18. Storms, fire and other damage

If a fire, storm, hurricane, flood or other disaster not caused by Tenant:
(a) **destroys** the Home or makes it unlivable, the lease ends and rent stops as of that date;
(b) **partly damages** it, Landlord repairs within a reasonable time, and rent is reduced fairly for the part Tenant can't use, or either party may end the lease if the Home can't reasonably be lived in.

Tenant follows evacuation orders and secures the Home and belongings before a storm when reasonably possible. Landlord's insurance does not cover Tenant's belongings. ☐ Renter's insurance is **recommended** / ☐ **required**, with at least **[$AMOUNT]** of liability coverage, proof due **[DATE]**.

*Legal basis: art. 2714 (total destruction ends the lease; no damages if no one is at fault). Art. 2715 (partial destruction: reduced rent or dissolution). R.S. 9:3260 (if the home becomes uninhabitable through no fault of the tenant, the landlord must mitigate).*

### 19. Default and remedies

Tenant is in default if Tenant (a) doesn't pay rent or other money due under this lease when due; (b) materially breaks this lease; (c) abandons the Home; (d) gave materially false information on the rental application; or (e) Tenant, an occupant or a guest commits criminal activity on or near the Home that threatens anyone's health or safety, or involves illegal drugs.

If Tenant defaults, **Landlord chooses ONE** of these:
1. **End the lease and evict.** Landlord recovers possession, plus rent and other amounts owed up to the date Landlord gets the Home back, and the cost of damage beyond normal wear. Landlord then gives up future rent. OR
2. **Keep the lease in force** and sue for rent as it comes due (or, ☐ if checked, for all remaining rent at once). Tenant then keeps the right to live in the Home.

**Waiver of notice to vacate (INITIAL: ______ / ______):** Tenant waives the five-day written notice to vacate in Code of Civil Procedure art. 4701. If Tenant's right to occupy ends for any reason, Landlord may immediately file for eviction in court.
☐ **Federally backed mortgage check:** if the Home has a mortgage backed by Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, FHA, VA or USDA, or is in a federal housing program, Landlord will give at least **30 days'** written notice to vacate before requiring Tenant to leave, and this waiver does not apply to that notice.

*Legal basis: arts. 2704 and 2719 (dissolution for nonpayment or breach). The choice of ONE remedy follows Louisiana case law: Lobell v. Rosenberg Denn, La. App. 4 Cir. No. 2018-CA-0600, quoting Richard v. Broussard, 495 So.2d 1291 (La. 1986) ("These remedies are mutually exclusive"). Leases A, B and C let the landlord do both, which a court will not enforce. CCP arts. 4701 ¶3 and 4731(A) (a written waiver in the lease is allowed). 15 U.S.C. 9058(c) (30-day notice for covered dwellings; statute read; whether courts still enforce it was not verified). Eviction is only through court: CCP arts. 4731-4733.*

### 20. Abandonment and items left behind

Landlord treats the Home as abandoned only when Landlord reasonably believes Tenant has left: for example, keys returned, belongings removed, or nobody living there, together with unpaid rent. Before taking possession without a court order, Landlord **posts a written notice on the door** (and sends one to Tenant's last known address) as Louisiana law requires. **In a parish under a federal disaster declaration, Tenant's absence during the first 30 days after the declaration is never treated as abandonment.** Items left after Tenant moves out or is lawfully evicted may be removed, stored, and disposed of after any notice the law requires. Reasonable costs may be deducted from the deposit.

*Legal basis: CCP art. 4731(B) (after the required notice, the landlord may take possession without court on a reasonable belief of abandonment; lists the signs). Art. 4731(C) (30-day disaster rule; penalty of $500 or 2× monthly rent, whichever is greater). CCP art. 4703 (notices may be attached to the door when the home is abandoned). This replaces Leases B/C §22 ("after 7 days … remove all belongings").*

### 21. Notices and electronic signatures

- **Everyday matters** (repair requests, entry notices, reminders) may be sent by text or email: Landlord **[PHONE / EMAIL]**; Tenant **[PHONE / EMAIL]**.
- **Formal notices** (ending the lease, notice to vacate, default, acceleration, right to cure, eviction, foreclosure) are given **on paper**: hand-delivered, mailed, or attached to the door when the law allows. Landlord's address: **[ADDRESS]**. Tenant's address: the Home (or the forwarding address after move-out). A copy may also be sent electronically.
- Notice to or from any one Tenant counts for all Tenants.
- The parties agree to sign this lease and its addenda electronically. Each party may still ask for paper for any later document.

*Legal basis: art. 2729 (termination notice for a house must be in writing). R.S. 9:2603(B)(4)(b)(ii) (Louisiana's electronic-transactions law does not apply to notices of default, acceleration, repossession, foreclosure, eviction or right to cure under a rental agreement for a primary residence). R.S. 9:2605(B)-(C) (electronic dealings need both sides' agreement; either may refuse later). R.S. 9:2607 (e-signatures are valid). 50 U.S.C. 3955(c)(2) (a servicemember may deliver notice electronically).*

### 22. Foreclosure disclosure

Tenant has the right to receive written notice of any foreclosure action against the Home.
☐ The Home is **not** currently subject to any pending foreclosure action.
☐ The Home **is** subject to a pending foreclosure action: **[COURT, CASE NAME, DOCKET NUMBER]**.

If Landlord is ever served with a notice of seizure in a foreclosure of the Home, Landlord will, within 7 calendar days, give every Tenant a signed written notice naming the court, case name and docket number, with this statement: *"This is not a notice to vacate the premises. This notice does not mean ownership of the building has changed. All lessees are still responsible for payment of rent and other obligations under the rental agreement. The lessor is still responsible for his obligations under the rental agreement. You will receive additional notice if there is a change in owner."*

*Legal basis: R.S. 9:3260.1(A)-(D) (required before and during every residential lease; $200 penalty). Missing from Leases B and C.*

### 23. Holdover

If Tenant stays after the lease has ended **and** Landlord has objected in writing, Tenant owes **[$AMOUNT per day / __% of daily rent]** for each day until Tenant leaves. This does not give Tenant any right to stay.

*Legal basis: a stipulated-damages term (arts. 2005-2012; keep it reasonable under art. 2012). Without a written objection the lease may instead reconduct month to month (art. 2721), which is why this clause requires one.*

### 24. Liability

Each party is responsible for harm caused by its own fault. Landlord is not responsible for loss of or damage to Tenant's belongings, or for injury, **except** as caused by Landlord's fault or by a defect Landlord is responsible for under Louisiana law. Tenant is responsible for, and will repay Landlord for, claims caused by Tenant, occupants, guests or pets. **Nothing in this lease limits anyone's liability for causing physical injury, or for intentional or gross fault.**

**Optional, tenant takes responsibility for the condition of the Home (INITIAL: ______ / ______):** Tenant assumes responsibility for the condition of the Home. Landlord is then not liable for injury caused by a defect in the Home to Tenant or anyone there through Tenant, **unless Landlord knew or should have known of the defect, or was told about it and did not fix it within a reasonable time.** This does not change Landlord's repair duties in Section 12.

*Legal basis: art. 2004 (any clause limiting liability in advance for physical injury, or for intentional or gross fault, is null). That is why Leases B/C §38 ("unless caused solely by Landlord's negligence") was replaced. R.S. 9:3221 (the optional assumption clause; the owner stays liable for defects known or that should have been known, or reported and not fixed). Art. 2698 (the warranty protects everyone living there under the lease).*

### 25. Fair housing and accommodations

Landlord follows federal and Louisiana fair-housing laws. Tenant may ask at any time for a reasonable accommodation (a change in a rule, policy or service) or a reasonable modification (a physical change to the Home) because of a disability. Landlord will not refuse a reasonable request. Modifications are at Tenant's expense, and Landlord may reasonably require that the inside of the Home be restored at move-out (normal wear excepted). Landlord does not retaliate against Tenant for asserting a legal right, asking for repairs, or contacting a government agency.

*Legal basis: 42 U.S.C. 3604(c) and (f)(3)(A)-(B). 24 CFR 100.202(c) (no asking applicants whether they have a disability) and 100.204. This replaces Leases B/C §36, which put modification costs on the landlord "unless too difficult" and invited tenants to disclose impairments. The Louisiana Equal Housing Opportunity Act (R.S. 51:2601+) was not read.*

### 26. Attorney's fees

If either party goes to court to enforce this lease, the winning party may recover reasonable attorney's fees and court costs.

*Legal basis: a contract term. R.S. 9:3259 gives statutory fees for unpaid rent only on oral leases, so a written lease needs its own clause. R.S. 9:3253 (court discretion on fees in deposit suits).*

### 27. General terms

- **Joint responsibility:** each Tenant is fully responsible for the whole lease, including all rent.
- **No waiver:** accepting late or partial rent does not waive any right, and a waiver counts only in writing signed by both parties.
- **Severability:** if any part is invalid, the rest stays in effect.
- **Entire agreement:** this lease and its addenda are the whole agreement. Changes count only in writing signed by both parties.
- **Good faith:** the parties will try in good faith to resolve disputes before going to court.
- **Death:** this lease does not end on the death of either party.
- **Governing law:** Louisiana.

*Legal basis: art. 1983 (a contract is law between the parties, performed in good faith). Art. 2717 (death does not end a lease). Art. 2726 (amendments).*

Landlord may revoke pet permission after written notice of a repeated, uncorrected violation. New pets need written OK.

**Part B: Assistance animals** (service animals and emotional-support animals)
- An assistance animal is **not a pet**. **No pet fee, pet deposit, pet rent, breed or weight limit applies.**
- Tenant may request one at any time. If the disability or the need for the animal is not obvious, Landlord may ask for reliable information showing the need. Landlord will not ask for a diagnosis or medical records.
- Tenant is still responsible for the animal's care and control, and for **actual** damage it causes beyond normal wear, like any other damage under the lease.
- Landlord may deny a specific animal only as fair-housing law allows (for example, a direct threat to others' safety that cannot be reduced, or serious property damage).

*Legal basis: 42 U.S.C. 3604(f)(3)(B). 24 CFR 100.204 (refusing a reasonable accommodation to a no-pets rule is unlawful; guide-dog example). 24 CFR 100.202(c)-(d) (limits on disability inquiries; direct-threat exception). HUD's current guidance on assistance-animal fees and documentation was not verified, so this addendum takes the cautious approach (no fees, no medical records).*
